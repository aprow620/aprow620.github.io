## Celebrating a Decade of Visitors’ Voices

Since 2015, the Nature Center has been able to welcome guests each season thanks to the dedication of our volunteers. Over these ten years, we’ve also been gathering reflections in our visitor log. While the log doesn’t capture every person who walks through our doors, it offers a unique snapshot of the many individuals who have experienced Clark Reservation and shared what the park means to them.

As a scientist who often works with large datasets, I’ve found that even small, human-recorded archives can offer valuable insights. This project with our visitor log was meant as a fun exercise, but it also shows how unexpected data sources can help us appreciate patterns of connection and diversity. My hope is that others will enjoy seeing the story behind the numbers and perhaps appreciate the ways simple records, like a guestbook entry, can capture something meaningful about our shared experience at Clark Reservation.

Of the nearly 1,500 entries in our visitor log, 1,430 included a place of origin. This simple detail offers a glimpse into the diversity of people who visit Clark Reservation.

From New York State, we have welcomed >1149 visitors from 48 of the 62 counties. This map offers an interesting perspective on travel in the state of NY. At no surprise, the highest density is from our home county of Onondaga, with the next greatest number of visitors originating from our neighbor in the north, Oswego County.

Zooming out to the entire United States, this is when I discovered [“Oneida County, Idaho”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneida_County,_Idaho), a reminder to be critical of any data.
Once corrected, the unusually high density of visitors from rural southeastern Idaho in my original analysis was resolved. Altogether, visitors from 41 states signed in, and some curious patterns emerged: for instance, we recorded more visitors from Houston, Texas, than from our much closer neighbor, New York City. Perhaps this says something about Texans' thirst for adventure.
